PLANNER DEVIATION on S-04 (knot angle set), evidence-based, executor must follow this version:
the spec wording says a new knot curve takes "the angle set of the nearest bracketing curve". A
prototype on the real X35 polar (scratch script, 2026-11-07) showed that rule breaks S-09/S-05:
with knots at 18 between the 16 and 20 curves, speeds below start move by up to 0.05 kn, and at
17.4-18 kn the upwind target angle jumps 38.0 -> 37.2 deg with a 1.1 % lower target speed, while
the factor is still 1. The knot instead uses the UNION of the two bracketing curves' TWA points
that are <= polar._deepest(k), plus polar._deepest(k) itself, with speeds polar.speed(k, a). That
reproduces the raw polar exactly at the knot (both bracketing curves are piecewise-linear with
breakpoints in that union), keeps the interpolated deepest TWA (so _deepest, which drives the
downwind targets, is unchanged at every TWS), keeps every existing curve's own points, and
degenerates to exactly the spec's "copy of the top curve" above the highest TWS (and to the
lowest curve's points below the lowest TWS). Prototype result with this rule, defaults 18/25/0.05:
speed error at TWS <= 18 kn = 1.8e-15, at >= 25 kn derated = 0.95 x raw within 1.8e-15, zero VMG
angle moves from 4 to 40 kn (0.1 kn steps), router PolarGrid rows 0-18 identical, rows 25 and 30
= 0.95 x raw, PolarGrid up_deg/down_deg identical on every row.

Facts the executor needs (read from the code, do not re-derive):
- Polar.__post_init__ sorts curves, inserts (0, 0) on every curve and an implicit TWS-0 curve;
  `polar.curves` afterwards holds the real curves only (TWS > 0), each including its (0.0, 0.0)
  point. Building a new Polar from such curves is safe (no duplicate (0, 0)).
- Polar.speed clamps above the highest TWS; Polar._deepest(tws) interpolates the last TWA of the

  angles, so union knots add no new candidate angles.
- The X35 builtin curves are 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 20 kn (14-20 all reach 180 deg). With the
